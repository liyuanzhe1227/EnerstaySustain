Preface

Medical devices connect technological innovation with human life and health. Packaging choices, supplier changes, clinical data and commercial activity can affect environmental burdens, patient safety, people’s rights and organisational governance at the same time. ESG offers a practical language for bringing these relationships into one evidence-aware view.

Grounded in the roles performed by medical-device companies and the product lifecycle, the book explains how to identify material topics, establish operational management systems, obtain reliable data and reach evidence-based evaluations. The objective is clearer responsibility, more timely risk response, more effective resource use and more credible communication.

The monograph is organised into six parts and twenty chapters. It moves from concepts and institutional context through management-system operation, material environmental, social and governance topics, data foundations, maturity evaluation, certification practice, integrated cases and international alignment.

Facts, requirements and evaluations are treated separately throughout the book. Facts establish what occurred, requirements define the criteria for judgement, and evaluations explain which conclusions the available evidence can support. Cases show both possible actions and ways to verify their effectiveness.

The principal audience includes management, quality, regulatory affairs, production, procurement, research and development, data and compliance personnel in medical-device companies, as well as university, corporate-training and professional evaluation cohorts.
