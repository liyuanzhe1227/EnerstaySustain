Preface

ESG is moving beyond public-welfare activity and corporate reporting to become part of operating capability, risk management, customer access, financing communication and long-term competitiveness. Companies increasingly need to explain how sustainability responsibilities are governed, evidenced and improved.

The book is written for chairpersons, general managers, ESG and marketing managers, supply-chain and compliance teams, and organisations developing international business. It connects corporate operations with the path from awareness and governance to risk, data, disclosure, assurance, market access and capability improvement.

Its emphasis is on both corporate context and management practice. ESG becomes an organisational capability only when it enters strategy, responsibilities, risk management, operational processes, supply-chain control, data systems, customer communication and continual improvement.

The learning sequence follows how companies actually implement ESG: external business and regulatory drivers; strategy, governance, risk and materiality; environmental, social and governance issue management; disclosure, assurance and verification; and an implementation roadmap supported by cases and practical tools.

Singapore and Southeast Asia provide an important application context, particularly for regional headquarters, green finance, carbon management and international market access. The book also addresses the practical needs of Chinese companies translating existing management capabilities into ESG language, data evidence and external trust.

Credible ESG information depends on clear data sources, responsibility boundaries, internal controls, evidence records and appropriate third-party review. Digital tools and artificial intelligence are discussed as enablers of this evidence chain rather than substitutes for sound governance and data definitions.
